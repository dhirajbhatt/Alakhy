About

The principal
behind Alakhy.

Dhiraj Bhatt. Technology architect and operator. Two decades building platforms that process transactions, move people and goods, and power enterprise operations across Africa, South Asia, and beyond.

Background

I build systems that operate in the real world. Not demos, not prototypes — platforms that process financial transactions at scale, coordinate logistics across unreliable infrastructure, and run enterprise operations across multiple geographies.

Currently Chief Architect and acting CTO at Credable, an embedded lending fintech operating in Tanzania, Kenya, Mozambique, and India. We run a single configurable platform that powers loans, savings, device financing, and BNPL for partner banks and telcos across four markets.

Before that, Chief Architect at KOKO Networks — building the B2C platform and farmer-facing systems that sit underneath a clean energy distribution network serving hundreds of thousands of households across East Africa.

Earlier, principal engineering and architecture roles at Rocket Software, SevOne, Symantec, and IBM Internet Security Systems. The common thread: systems where failure is not an option and the engineering has to hold.

I founded Alakhy to bring that depth to bear independently — without the conflicts, handoffs, or dilution that come with scale. Technology due diligence, architecture advisory, fractional CTO, hiring — the work that requires someone who has actually built platforms, not just consulted on them.

I am also building HireLens — an AI-native ATS for teams who make technical hires — because I have been on both sides of technical hiring long enough to know it is broken.

Credable

Credable is the platform behind the credit product. One transaction engine. Partners — banks, telcos, retailers — get different products by toggling configuration, not by writing new services.

Four markets. Multiple partners per market. Loans, savings, device financing, and BNPL — all on the same architecture. Every customer journey maps to three steps: identity and eligibility, product execution, settlement and reconciliation.

The engineering challenge is keeping that clean as the product surface expands. That is the work.
